# A Conversation on Self-Realization, Shipped as Code: `famoroti-solar-system` Released Open-Source on PyPI

### Richard A. Famoroti reimagines his 2017 Java project as a clean, test-backed Python package — treating disciplined building as the practical answer to avoiding failure.

**[CITY, Country] — June 13, 2026 —** What began as a recorded conversation with a YouTube creator — whose work reaches roughly 14,000 viewers a month — about self-realization as the key to avoiding failure now has something most conversations never produce: a finished, working artifact. Developer Richard A. Famoroti has released `famoroti-solar-system`, a free and open-source Python package, as the deliberate companion to that discussion.

The idea behind the talk was simple. Knowing yourself, and building from your own first principles rather than borrowed ones, is what keeps work from collapsing. Famoroti chose to demonstrate it instead of only describing it. He took a solar-system model he first wrote in Java in 2017 and rebuilt it from the ground up in Python — not as a copy, but as a faithful re-imagining that preserves the original's principles while expressing them idiomatically in their new language.

The result is small on purpose. `famoroti-solar-system` models a "fantasy" solar system through two well-encapsulated classes. A `Planet` validates its own data and computes its orbital period directly from Kepler's third law (period in years equals the square root of distance in AU, cubed). A `SolarSystem` is a named, iterable collection of planets that accepts new worlds however you hand them over. An interactive builder lets anyone generate and tabulate their own system from the command line. The package ships with a 19-test suite, an MIT license, and standard Python packaging — the quiet markers of work that was finished, not just started.

"Self-realization isn't an abstraction you talk about — it's something you prove by building something true to its own principles, and finishing it," said Richard A. Famoroti. "This package is that proof, made small enough to hold."

## Availability

`famoroti-solar-system` is available now, free and open-source under the MIT license:

- **Install:** `pip install famoroti-solar-system`
- **Source:** https://github.com/Richadaf/python_solar-system
- **Package:** https://pypi.org/project/famoroti-solar-system/

It imports as `solar_system` and installs a `fantasy-solar-system` console command.
